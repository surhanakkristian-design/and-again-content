# Tinder texts (English): the rules

The And Again app shows a media (image or short video) with a sentence; the learner swipes right if the
sentence is TRUE for the media and left if it is FALSE. Learners are 15-25, levels A1-B2. Every text is
later translated into 8 languages (sk, cs, de, es, fr, uk, tr, hu).

Every media gets FOUR texts:

## 1. TRUE sentence and 2. FALSE sentence (max 60 characters each, counted with spaces and punctuation)
- GRAMMAR FIRST. Articles, agreement, prepositions, tense must be flawless. A funny sentence with a mistake
  is worse than a plain correct one. Complete sentences with capital letter and end punctuation.
- Tense follows reality. An action that is going on in the video or frozen mid-action in the picture is
  happening NOW: use the PRESENT CONTINUOUS ("The panda is hugging her.", "She is closing the door.").
  The present simple is ONLY for states and stative verbs (is, has, likes, wants, looks, needs, knows),
  habits and general facts ("He lifts every day.", "Cats hate water."). "The turtle goes into the sea." for
  a clip of a turtle walking into the sea is WRONG: it must be "The turtle is going into the sea."
  PAST for an action the media shows as finished or whose result is visible ("The dog stole the sausage.",
  "Somebody ate half the cake."); FUTURE for something clearly about to happen ("He will fall!", "She is
  going to jump."). Do not choose a tense to save characters; shorten elsewhere.
- The target word MUST appear in the TRUE sentence, in any natural form (plural, past tense, -ing, etc.),
  in the sense given by its definition.
- SHORT: between 76 % and 85 % of your TRUE sentences must be 30 characters or less (the owner's floor is
  70 %, counted per level). Use the remaining 15-24 % (up to 60 characters) for the best jokes.
  A short, clean factual sentence is a good outcome when the media offers nothing funny.
- HUMOUR SECOND, BUT PRESENT: aim for a humour device in at least half of the media. Short sentences can
  carry a joke too: exclamation ("The monkey stole my banana!"), understatement ("Just a small wave."),
  guessing ("He will regret this."), blaming ("The cat did it."), cheap_quality ("Cheap tent, big storm.").
  Vary the tones: no single tone above 35 % of your slice.
- Never invent anything the description/transcript does not show (no invented names, colours, objects,
  outcomes, feelings that are not visible). Guessing what someone THINKS or what WILL happen is allowed
  when phrased as a guess/joke that the scene clearly invites.
- If nothing in the media is funny, write a short clean factual sentence (device "none"). That is better
  than a forced joke.
- Humour devices, varied; never the same device twice in a row within your slice (except "none"):
  exaggeration, understatement, cliche, bad_excuse, blaming_someone, banter (light men/women banter, only
  where the media shows both or clearly invites it; never about looks, bodies or intelligence),
  cheap_quality (jokes about cheap things / bad quality), guessing (what happens next / what someone
  thinks), exclamation (the emotion carries an exclamation mark), none.
- Stretching a sentence past 30 characters only to fit a joke: at most 2 in 10.
- Pop culture where it genuinely fits: only names a wide European audience knows (Harry Potter, the
  Simpsons, Spider-Man, the Avengers, Barbie, Baywatch, Messi, Ronaldo, LeBron, Taylor Swift, Beyonce,
  Brad Pitt, Margot Robbie, Kim Kardashian, Shrek, Titanic, Star Wars, Batman, Rocky, Mr Bean, Gordon
  Ramsay and the like). Nothing niche, nothing political, never mock a living person (comparing someone's
  action favourably or playfully is fine: "He kicks like Ronaldo!"). Use it sparingly, maybe 1 in 15.
- Irony: put the ironic word in double quotes when it could be read as fact (Such "brave" parents!); no
  quotes when the irony is obvious.
- Register: informal is fine; shit, sucks, asshole, damn allowed (rarely). NEVER slut, shut up, dick,
  cock, fuck or any slur. No sexual content, nothing about anyone's body or looks, nothing mocking a
  nationality, religion, race or disability. No violence or injuries as a joke.
- NO English wordplay, puns, rhymes, idioms whose meaning depends on English words: it must survive
  translation into 8 languages.
- FALSE sentence: same tone, same length class (if TRUE is <=30, FALSE is <=30 too; otherwise <=60),
  grammatically perfect, same structure as TRUE, wrong on exactly ONE checkable fact (a swapped noun,
  colour, number, place, animal or action) that a learner sees at a glance in the media. It must not be
  true by accident, and not be a mere opinion. The target word may appear or not in FALSE.

## 3. TRUE short phrase (max 30 characters, 2-5 words, NOT a sentence)
- The shortest exact label for what is happening, built around the target word. The word is always there
  and never stands alone: "to get down under the bar", "to get away from it all", "a pig winning the race",
  "a closed garage door", "smelling the flowers".
- Natural English: keep the article or the infinitive "to" where the phrase needs it. No capital letter at
  the start (unless a proper name), no full stop.

## 4. FALSE short phrase (same shape, max 30 characters, 2-5 words)
- Must NOT fit the media, but look similar at first glance: build it from the SAME key word in another
  sense or with another continuation ("to get away with it"), or from the same particle, object or setting
  seen in the media ("to look down at the bar"). After a moment's thought it is clearly wrong.
- Never wrong because of grammar, never absurd or nonsensical ("a panda's tight hat" is absurd), never
  trivially unrelated. The learner must look at the media to decide. The same applies to the FALSE
  sentence: the swapped fact must be plausible in principle ("lifts a star" is absurd; "lifts a rock" works).

## Tone (one per media, the app's nine styles)
chill, slang, ironic, dramatic, business, gossip, nerd, flirt, low_iq.
Gates: nerd only B1/B2/B; ironic and gossip not for A1; low_iq not for B2; flirt only between adults and
only complimenting actions or style; business: no exclamation marks, no slang, no real people or brands;
chill: no exclamation marks, no slang. The tone must be visible in the sentence: a plain factual sentence is always chill; label business, gossip,
ironic etc. only when the wording really carries that tone. Vary the tones through the wording, not the label.

## Tense label
present_continuous, present_simple, past, future (the tense of the TRUE sentence's main verb; for a
verbless sentence use the one it implies, or present_simple).
