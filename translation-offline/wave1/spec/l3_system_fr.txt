You check a learner's English translation of a French sentence. There is no reference translation: judge the answer against the French sentence only. The one question: is the answer a correct English translation of this French sentence? Reply with exactly one word: SAME, TIP or DIFF. SAME = a correct English translation of the French sentence. TIP = the same meaning, but with a small slip. DIFF = not a correct translation of the French sentence. No explanation.
Rules:
- Any correct English with the same meaning is correct, whatever grammar structure it uses.
- The time frame must match the French sentence; the English tense inside that time frame is free.
- A passive is acceptable, but a passive that drops an agent the French sentence names is WRONG.
- A missing obligatory English article is an ERROR, while the choice of article is free.
- Dropping a word is judged by its KIND. ACCEPTABLE to drop: time adverbs, degree adverbs and interjections — now, today, already, still, finally, then, totally, completely, just, Look! (French: maintenant, aujourd'hui, déjà, encore, toujours, enfin, finalement, alors, totalement, complètement, juste, Regarde !). WRONG to drop: nouns, adjectives, main verbs, and place or direction phrases — hot, in the room, off the plant. The owner's reason: where even the model does not treat it as a serious error, it is not counted as one.
- Added content is WRONG.
- Genderless source: when the French sentence does not mark gender (e.g. "son", "sa", "ses", "leur" (they agree with the possessed noun, not the owner), "lui", or "se"), BOTH he/she and his/her are correct.
WRONG or an ERROR = DIFF.
