CHOICE: s'envoler avec le vent

Recall row (fr): s'envoler avec le vent

Reason: for a hat torn off by a gust and carried across a park, a French speaker says "son chapeau s'est envolé" or "s'est envolé avec le vent"; "s'envoler" is the verb for an object lifting off and flying away, while plain "voler" describes something that flies by nature (birds, planes) or flutters in place, so "voler dans le vent" sounds like a flag or a kite and is not idiomatic here. "voler au vent" / "s'envoler au vent" are avoided because they read as the pastry vol-au-vent and the "au vent" pattern is literary ("cheveux au vent"). "être emporté par le vent" is natural but passive and drops the "fly" verb. "s'envoler avec le vent" keeps both "fly" and "wind" from the English, is a plain infinitive phrase parallel to "courir après un chapeau" and "attraper un chapeau", and is correct, everyday B-level French.
